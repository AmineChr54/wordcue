# Pacing and on-screen writing

**Always, in any style**
- Give every figure a label saying what it measures, and compare like with like.
- Say estimates out loud and show them in readable type.
- Keep what's on screen distinct from the narration, so the viewer isn't reading along with the voice.
- Know what the viewer should remember, and make it easy to find at the end.

**Starting points, drawn from one fast film. Change them when the project wants something else**
- Open on something concrete, such as a person, a time or a real artefact.
- Something changes every 3-5 seconds, faster in the opening. A calm film can hold far longer if the stillness is intended.
- One idea per shot. A zoom holds at least 1.5 seconds.
- Big type lands about 0.1 seconds before its word.
- Callouts are 2-3 words.
- Show data as a picture at one true scale.
- Reuse an early frame at the turn, so the viewer sees the difference.
- End on the takeaway, with the reason next to it.
- Name the product by the turning point.
- Silence is allowed. A reveal can play with no voice for 1-2 seconds.
