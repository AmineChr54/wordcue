# Self-check before review

These faults survived into full renders of earlier films. They concern truth and legibility, so they apply in any style. Look at real frames, not the source.

**Claims**
- Every figure, time and name traces to the source, or is labelled as staged or estimated.
- Wording keeps the source's scope. "Spread across a week" and "more than a week" are different claims.
- Motion makes claims too. A line running through every item says the thing touches every item.
- Narration and screen agree. If a line says "an hour later", the screen can't show the same hour.
- If the source contradicts itself, pick one value and tell the user.
- A demonstration must show the claim. Check that the thing shown failing isn't the same thing later offered as the cure.
- Say what the picture shows. If a number's on screen, the voice and the type must match, and rounding gets a label.

**The message**
- Read the last frame as a stranger who saw the film once. Two figures at the same size read as a comparison, so make sure it's the intended one.
- Compare like with like, and give each figure a label saying what it measures.
- Name the product early enough that the change has a cause.

**Timing**
- A clock, counter or stamp reaches its value on the word that says it.
- Cause comes before effect. Anything that changes because of something must change as, or after, that thing is shown.
- Cards or titles that change quickly must each match the word being spoken at that moment.
- Time passing on screen has something happening in it, or it reads as idle.
- Check every long hold: does the picture give a reason to keep looking, or is the stillness intended?

**Layout**
- Check the corner pixels of a real frame for a stray band or margin.
- Labels shown to the viewer can't be internal IDs, and any number on screen must be real.
- Pull a frame at the tightest point of every zoom. Labels and headers get cropped there.
- Check text against whatever passes over it, such as lines, panels and moving objects.
- After changing stacking order, check what the change hid. A fix here often broke something else.
- A label replaced in motion must be gone, not sitting under its replacement.
- Illustrations must look deliberate. Empty boxes and hatching can read as placeholders or redaction.
- Check drawings for sense, such as an impact on the right object.
